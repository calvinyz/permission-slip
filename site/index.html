    How it works

    A language model reads the agent's messages and turns them into a structured request. Every approval comes from rules checking verified facts.

    1. The agent asks

      By text or as a Claude connector, with what it wants to buy, the price and why.

    2. Claims are checked

      The meeting against the calendar, the fare against recent prices, the employee against the directory.

    3. Policy decides

      Cabin class, fare limits and team budgets, in code. Over budget goes to a manager.

    4. A single-use card

      A signed mandate records the approval. The card works for that merchant category and amount only.

    5. Matched and coded

      When the charge settles it is matched to its approval and coded to the right GL account.

    Why the model can't approve spend

    Approval has to happen before the purchase, and it has to hold up when an agent misreads a request or gets manipulated.

    Decisions come from code

    Every decision comes from deterministic rules with a rule-by-rule trace. No model is in the approval path.

    Claims are verified

    A meeting the agent mentions must be on the calendar. A fare must be in line with recent prices for the route.

    Limits stay private

    Agents learn whether a request is approved, never the cap they could price up to.

    Approvals are signed

    Each approval is an Ed25519-signed mandate. Its card works only for that merchant category and amount, and freezes on a mismatch.

    Architecture

    The conversation service talks to agents. The gateway makes every decision and moves every card. They share only an OpenAPI contract.
